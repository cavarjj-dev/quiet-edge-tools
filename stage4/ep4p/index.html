The Post-Game Delay

The ride home is a decompression container. Not a debrief.

The conversation has value. The question is when it lands. In the first 45 to 60 minutes, the same note gets attached to the feeling, not the adjustment.

Joan Duda, University of Birmingham. Parent-initiated performance evaluation right after competition raises anxiety and cuts motivation, even when the tone is supportive.

1. The ride home rule

For the next three weeks, the car is free of performance analysis. Win or lose. You can connect as a parent. That is not the same as reviewing the game.

You look distracted out there.
What was going through your head in the third quarter?
When you go quiet like that, you lose your energy.
What went wrong?

Try one of these. Then nothing.

2. Put the analysis somewhere else

Write what you noticed before you say it. That is so it does not surface on the drive home.

3. When the window opens

Not in the car. Not in the first hour. If the note is still worth having the next day, that is when the same feedback can land. Frame it as what you observed. Not as a verdict on their energy, their quiet, or the result.

Week 1 will feel incomplete. Week 3 is when the car starts to feel like a safe zone instead of a review room.
