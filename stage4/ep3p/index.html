The Zone Witness Protocol

Parent Observation Kit — Self-Assessment, 3-Week Witness Log & Zone Signature Builder

What's Your Sideline Default?

5 questions · takes about 3 minutes · no right answers.
Answer based on what you actually do at the sideline — not what you think you should do.
Question 1 of 5
Your athlete is warming up before a game. They look more scattered or intense than most of their teammates. What's your first instinct?
Question 2 of 5
You notice that the athlete next to yours looks visibly focused and calm before the game. What happens in your head?
Question 3 of 5
Your athlete has a different pregame ritual from everyone else on the team. How do you feel about that?
Question 4 of 5
Before a big game, your athlete seems visibly intense — more wound up than usual. What do you do?
Question 5 of 5
After a strong game, if someone asked you to describe what your athlete's pregame looked like before that performance — could you?

3-Week Zone Witness Log

One entry per competition. Your only job: observe your athlete's pregame — not against others, against themselves. What do you see before a strong game? Fill this out on game day, before the competition begins.
The Witness Rule: No comparing. Watch your athlete — energy level, body language, demeanor, focus cues — and record what you see. You're building a map of their personal zone signature. Three weeks of entries gives you the data to reflect back to them.

Zone Signature Builder

After 3+ log entries, look across your observations for patterns. What do you consistently see before strong performances? This is the map you can reflect back to your athlete.
You're not looking for a prescription. You're looking for a pattern. If your athlete's pregame energy level is consistently 7–8 before strong performances — that's data. Not something to change. Something to recognize and name back to them: "You look exactly like you did before the conference game."
Energy Pattern
Looking across your log entries where the game went well — what was their typical pregame energy level? Did it cluster in a range? Was it consistent or variable?
Movement & Body Language Pattern
What do you see consistently in their body language before performances that go well? What looks different before a difficult one?
Focus & Social Pattern
How do they interact with teammates before a strong game? Do they go quiet, stay social, or something else? Is there a ritual or sequence you notice?
One-Word Tone Pattern
Looking at the words or phrases you used to describe their pregame tone across entries — is there a word that comes up most for strong performances?
What Surprises You
Looking at your entries, was anything surprising? A pattern you didn't expect? Something that contradicts what you thought their ideal pregame looked like?

Check-In Script

The single most valuable replacement for comparison language is one question. Everything else flows from it.
"You know your head better than I do.
What does it feel like right now?"
The Most Common Sideline Moment
Instead of
"Look how focused Jordan is — take a breath, try to get where she is."
Say
"You know your head better than I do. What does it feel like right now?"
Why it works: you're not ignoring the moment. You're giving them themselves as the reference instead of someone else. One question trains the habit of internal check-in. Over a season, that habit is the foundation of self-regulation.
Variations — Match to Your Athlete
"What does your head feel like?" (brief athletes — stop there) Works for athletes who don't want much pregame conversation. The question is the connection.
"How are you going in?" Even shorter. Opens the door without pressure. Some athletes answer; some don't — both responses tell you something.
"How does this compare to [strong game they both remember]?" Uses their own history as the reference — not another athlete. Grounds the comparison in their personal signature instead of someone else's.
"Anything you need from me, or are you set?" Preserves their autonomy. Most athletes say "I'm set" — which is the right answer, and you've connected without interfering.
(No question — hand on shoulder, then nothing) For athletes who signal "I'm in my process, don't interrupt." The presence without words is the right move. Build this read over 3 weeks of observation.
Language to Remove from Your Pregame Vocabulary
"Look how focused [name] is — try to be more like that." Gives them a borrowed benchmark. They start optimizing for what they look like instead of what works for them. Repeated across a season, it replaces internal self-knowledge with external reference.
"Everyone else seems ready — are you okay?" Uses the group as the standard. Also implies something might be wrong — which increases anxiety in athletes who were actually fine.
"You need to calm down / settle / lock in." Prescribes a state without asking what they actually need. Often pushes athletes away from what was working pregame.
"You seem scattered / distracted / off." Labels their internal state from the outside. Athletes frequently experience this as invalidating — especially when "scattered" is how they look when they're actually in their zone.
When to override the protocol: The Zone Witness Protocol is for normal pregame observation — not a rule that overrides your judgment as a parent. If your athlete is showing signs of genuine distress, acute anxiety, or something that concerns you beyond a competitive pregame, that conversation doesn't wait for a check-in question. Trust what you know about your child.
