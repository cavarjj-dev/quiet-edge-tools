The Process Contract

One behavior you control. Written before the session. Returned to when results pull.

Stage 4 · Ep. 4 · Athlete

Process focus is not positive thinking. It is not telling yourself to stop worrying about the result. It is one execution cue you can actually control, chosen before the pressure arrives.

Sian Beilock, University of Chicago. Evaluative pressure makes athletes over-monitor skills that were already automatic. That is the interruption. Not a loss of skill.

1. The contract

Before the next practice or game, name one behavior. Not a result. Not a stat. One thing you can do while the ball is live.

I need to make this.
Don't blow it.
What does he think of my game right now?

2. When results pull

They will. You already decided where attention goes. You do not make that decision in the moment. Return to the one behavior.

After the session, review the behavior. Not the outcome. If the line you wrote was a result, rewrite it before the next session. That is the calibration.

3. Three weeks

Run step 1 before every session. Week 1 will not feel like a shift. Week 3 is where a default starts. If a showcase is coming, build this before it, not during it.
